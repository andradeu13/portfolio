Le PetitE-commerceUX / UIDesign thinking

A shop a mom can
trust in one hand.

A mobile-first online store for children’s clothing in Ecuador. I designed it around how a parent really buys: by her child’s age, with doubts about payment, delivery and sizes, usually from her phone and often through WhatsApp.

RoleUX / UI design
MarketEcuador
FocusMobile-first, trust
PlatformCustom storefront + WooCommerce admin

Context & challenge

Selling online where trust is the real product

Online shopping in Ecuador still runs on trust: many buyers prefer to talk to someone on WhatsApp, delivery addresses are described rather than formal, and bank transfer sits next to cards. The brief was to make a store that looks premium and loads fast, but above all removes the moments where a parent hesitates and leaves.

Business goal

More sales, less friction

Turn visits into orders without adding more steps, and make every campaign measurable.

User goal

Buy the right size, safely

Find clothes for her child’s age, be sure they are in stock, and know exactly what she pays and when it arrives.

Constraint

Admin stays in WordPress

The team keeps managing products in WooCommerce while the storefront can be redesigned without touching it.

01 · Empathize

Who we are designing for

Persona and empathy map are a design synthesis built from the brief and from how this kind of shopper behaves. They are working hypotheses to be validated with interviews and store analytics, not research results.

C

Carolina, 32

Working mom in Quito. Shops on her phone between tasks.

Goal
Dress her child for the right age and season without wasting time.
Context
Arrives from Instagram or a WhatsApp message, one hand on the phone, interrupted often.
Fear
Paying on a website nobody she knows has used, and then hearing the size was out of stock.
Habit
Asks on WhatsApp first. Pays by transfer when she is not sure about cards.

Thinks

  • Is this shop real and will it arrive?
  • Will this size fit my child now, not in six months?
  • Where do I tap to pay without making a mistake?

Feels

  • Wary of paying online
  • Rushed, short on time
  • Relieved when someone answers or a step is clear

Says

  • “¿Tienen talla 4?”
  • “¿Cuánto cuesta el envío a mi casa?”
  • “Te mando el comprobante por WhatsApp.”

Does

  • Shops by her child’s age
  • Compares, then asks a person
  • Abandons forms that feel long or confusing

02 · Define

Pain points across the buying journey

Mapping the journey exposed nine friction points. Each one is written as a “How might we…” question that later became a concrete design decision.

Discover

I don’t know this shop

A new brand from an ad has no history. Without proof that real people bought, she hesitates before the first tap.

How might we…show real customers and a human channel before she has to commit?
Browse

Categories don’t match how I think

Product types (tops, pants) make her scan everything. She thinks “what does a 3-year-old need?”.

How might we…organise the catalog the way a parent decides: by age?
Browse

Lost in a long list

On desktop, long listings and many options overwhelm and she can’t tell what she has already filtered.

How might we…keep control visible and make it easy to start over?
Decide

Sizes and stock uncertainty

Finding out after paying that a size is gone is the worst moment in any store.

How might we…make it impossible to buy what isn’t there?
Decide

Hidden delivery and exchange rules

If shipping cost, sizing guide or exchanges are hard to find, she assumes the worst.

How might we…answer those doubts on the product page itself?
Cart

Losing my place

Leaving the page to review the cart breaks the flow and many never come back.

How might we…confirm the add and keep her exactly where she was?
Checkout

Long forms and ambiguous addresses

Addresses in Ecuador are often described (“cerca de…”), and tall forms feel like work.

How might we…make the form feel short and still deliver to the right door?
Pay & wait

Fear of paying on the web, and of paying twice

Some don’t trust card payment online. Others reload the page while the payment confirms and risk double charges.

How might we…offer a path for those who don’t trust the web, and calm the waiting?
After

Coming back is tedious

Forgotten passwords and re-typing data make the second purchase harder than the first.

How might we…make returning customers faster than first-time ones?

05 · Test & learn

Measuring where people drop

A good decision is one you can check. The store sends the same events from the browser and from the server, so a purchase is counted once even if the buyer blocks cookies, and every step of the funnel can be compared.

View product Which products and categories attract interest
Add to cart Does stock, sizing guide and trust copy convince?
Begin checkout Is the cart panel clear enough to continue?
Purchase Where the form, shipping cost or payment lose people
  • Meta Pixel + Conversions API and GA4 + Google Tag Manager with the same events, so campaigns optimise on real purchases and can be used for remarketing.
  • Loaded lazily, after the page appears or on interaction, so measurement does not slow the store.
  • Privacy policy updated to describe these tools.
  • Next experiments: test the position of the WhatsApp button, the cart panel copy and the shipping cost reveal, and compare funnel steps before and after each change.

Reliability

Trust is also what the user can’t see

Some decisions are invisible but protect the experience: they remove the failures that would break the trust the interface works to build.

  • Payments are marked paid only when the payment gateway confirms, never because the buyer returned to the page.
  • Server-side validation, origin checks and rate limits to prevent abuse.
  • Security headers and a strict content policy that allow only the domains the store needs.
  • Resilient transfer details: if bank data can’t be read from WooCommerce, fallback details are shown so the buyer is never left without them.

Impact on the brand

What this means for Le Petit

Sales

More sales, less friction

Fewer visible steps, fewer errors, real stock and clear notices remove the usual reasons to abandon.

Trust

Confidence at every step

Visible payment details, card logos, kind messages, a detailed confirmation and WhatsApp always at hand.

Brand

A premium feel

Speed, a clean and consistent interface, plus favicon and testimonials carrying the brand’s identity.

Growth

Decisions with data

With Meta and Google connected, every campaign can be measured, and the storefront can evolve without touching the admin.

What I would do next. Interview customers to validate the persona, review the funnel by step after launch, and run A/B tests on the changes above. Results will be added here when they are available.
