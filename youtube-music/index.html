YouTube Music
Redesigned.

Three UX problems that make daily use frustrating, and three design decisions that fix them.

Type Speculative redesign
Platform Mobile + Web
Tools Figma · Illustrator
View full project Behance ↗
Disclaimer: This is a personal UX concept project, not affiliated with or endorsed by YouTube or Google. All UI representations are illustrative redesign proposals only.

Why YouTube Music

A powerful product held back
by three daily frustrations.

YouTube Music has the biggest catalog of any streaming app, but the interface was unattractive, cluttered, and made it hard to enjoy even simple actions like finding or searching music. Users found the hierarchy confusing across both web and mobile. I redesigned it with a cleaner layout, clearer icons, and a modern color palette that feels simple, accessible, and visually engaging.

Problem 01
Interface is cluttered, no clear priority
Both the mobile and web interfaces dump too much on screen at once. No visual hierarchy, no filters, no clear entry point, users felt overwhelmed.
Problem 02
Library mixes everything
Songs, albums, playlists, music videos, and podcasts all live in one flat list. No way to filter by type without scrolling.
Problem 03
Player wastes the screen
Album art takes 60% of the screen, then there's empty space before the controls. Queue, lyrics, and related content are buried.
01
Home Discovery

Clean the home screen hierarchy.

The original home dumped everything at once, no clear priority, no visual breathing room. The redesign introduces filter chips (All / Music / Playlist) so users immediately narrow scope, and restructures content into clear sections: Recommended first (large card), then Recently Played as a scannable list. The information hierarchy now matches how people actually use the app.

Before Cluttered, no visual hierarchy
→
After Cleaner hierarchy, clear filters
02
Library

One library. Four clear categories.

The current library dumps everything together, music playlists, auto-mixes, music videos, and uploads in a single scrollable list. The redesign adds tab-based filtering at the top: Songs, Albums, Artists, Playlists, Videos. The user picks their mode and sees only what's relevant. Simple, obvious, fast.

Before All content types mixed
→
After Tab filter → instant clarity
03
Now Playing

Make the player screen earn its space.

The current player uses most of the screen for album art and leaves the bottom empty. Queue, lyrics, and related tracks are hidden behind gestures users don't know exist. The redesign compacts the art slightly and surfaces an "Up Next" mini-queue and quick-action buttons (lyrics, share) directly on the screen, no hidden gestures required.

Before Empty space, hidden queue
→
After Queue + actions always visible

What changed

Three problems. Three decisions.
One clearer product.

Home: Cluttered → Clear hierarchy
↓
Filter chips (All / Music / Playlist) + structured sections (Recommended → Recently Played) give the home screen a logical, scannable flow.
Library: Mixed content → Tabbed filtering
↓
Songs, Albums, Artists, Playlists, and Videos each get their own view. Find what you're looking for in one tap.
Player: Wasted space → Functional screen
↓
Compact art + always-visible Up Next queue + quick-access buttons for lyrics and sharing. No hidden gestures.

Takeaway

Great catalogs deserve
better navigation.

The most valuable thing this project reinforced: UX problems are rarely about missing features. YouTube Music has lyrics, has a queue, has discovery algorithms, but they're all buried. The redesign doesn't add anything new. It surfaces what's already there, in the moment users actually want it. That's the job.
